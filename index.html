Why Proper Segregation Matters

🌊 Prevents Clogged Canals & Flooding

Unsegregated plastics, sachets, and food scraps swept into street storm drains block waterways. Proper sorting keeps barangay esteros and drainage canals clear during heavy rains and typhoons.

🦟 Reduces Disease & Pests

Separating wet organic food waste from dry recyclables eliminates foul odors and stops breeding grounds for dengue-carrying mosquitoes, flies, and rodents in residential alleys.

⚖️ Complies with RA 9003

Under the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003), mandatory source segregation is enforced at the household level—unsegregated garbage is strictly subject to "No Segregation, No Collection."

The 4 Waste Categories Explained

GREEN BIN • NABUBULOK

🌱 Biodegradable

Organic materials that naturally decompose into nutrient-rich soil.

  • Fruit & vegetable peelings
  • Leftover food, fish & meat scraps
  • Dry leaves, twigs & garden trimmings
  • Eggshells & coffee grounds
BLUE BIN • NARERESIKLO

♻️ Recyclable

Clean, dry materials that can be recovered and processed at the MRF.

  • PET plastic bottles & clean HDPE jugs
  • Cardboard boxes, newspapers & office paper
  • Aluminum soda cans & tin cans
  • Intact glass bottles & jars
BLACK / GRAY BIN • RESIDUAL

🗑️ Residual Waste

Non-recyclable, non-compostable items bound for sanitary landfills.

  • Shampoo, coffee & condiment sachets
  • Soiled food wrappers & greasy boxes
  • Used diapers, sanitary napkins & tissues
  • Styrofoam, worn-out rags & ceramics
RED BIN • SPECIAL / HAZARDOUS

☣️ Hazardous / E-Waste

Toxic or electronic items requiring specialized handling and containment.

  • Used batteries, busted chargers & cables
  • Broken LED/fluorescent bulbs & appliances
  • Expired medicines & paint/solvent cans
  • Pesticide bottles & aerosol sprays

Composting & MRF Drop-off Tips

🪴 Backyard & Sack Composting

  • Layer Greens & Browns: Mix wet kitchen scraps (nitrogen) with dry leaves, shredded cardboard, or sawdust (carbon) at a 1:2 ratio to prevent odor.
  • Aerate Weekly: Turn the compost pile or perforated sack once a week and keep it moist like a wrung-out sponge.
  • Harvest in 45–60 Days: Dark, earthy-smelling soil is ready for barangay urban gardens.

🏭 Materials Recovery Facility (MRF) Prep

  • Rinse & Dry: Quickly rinse food residue off bottles, cans, and tubs so they don't contaminate paper recyclables.
  • Flatten & Bundle: Flatten cardboard boxes and crush plastic bottles to save storage space at home and in collection trucks.
  • Keep E-Waste Separate: Never mix batteries or busted bulbs with regular trash; hand them directly to the MRF attendant.
